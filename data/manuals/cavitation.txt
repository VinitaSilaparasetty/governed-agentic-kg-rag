TITLE: Impeller Cavitation — Detection, Root Cause, and Corrective Actions
SOURCE: Industrial Maintenance Manual Rev. 4.2, Section 8.1

SECTION 1: WHAT IS CAVITATION
Cavitation occurs when the local static pressure in the pump inlet falls below the vapour
pressure of the pumped liquid, causing vapour bubbles to form. These bubbles collapse
violently as they enter the high-pressure impeller zone, releasing energy that erodes
metal surfaces and generates broadband vibration. Left uncorrected, cavitation causes
pitting and material loss on impeller vanes and the volute, eventually causing through-wall
failure and unplanned downtime.

SECTION 2: SYMPTOMS
- Random high-frequency broadband vibration with a crackling or gravel-like acoustic
  signature (recorded on accelerometer spectrum 1–20 kHz).
- Step-change reduction in flow rate or discharge pressure without change in speed.
- Pitting marks visible on impeller vane inlet edges during inspection.
- Increased radial bearing load due to flow imbalance.

SECTION 3: NPSH ANALYSIS
The Net Positive Suction Head available (NPSHa) must exceed the pump's required NPSH
(NPSHr) by at least 0.5 m margin. Calculate NPSHa as:
  NPSHa = (Patm – Pvapour) / ρg + hz – hL
where hz is suction static head and hL is friction losses in suction piping.
If NPSHa < NPSHr + 0.5 m, the pump is operating in a cavitating regime.

SECTION 4: CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
A. Reduce speed (VFD setpoint) to lower NPSHr — cavitation intensity falls as the cube
   of speed reduction.
B. Lower suction piping friction losses: check inlet strainer for fouling (>30% blockage
   triggers cavitation), replace with larger-bore strainer, shorten suction pipe runs.
C. Lower fluid temperature to reduce vapour pressure (relevant for hot condensate pumps).
D. Increase suction tank level or pump inlet elevation.
E. If impeller shows visible pitting >2 mm depth, replace impeller. See Section 7.3 for
   impeller removal procedure.

SECTION 5: PREVENTION
Operate within the Preferred Operating Region (70–120% BEP). Install vibration monitors
with threshold alarms at 3 mm/s and shutdown at 9 mm/s. Inspect suction strainer monthly.
