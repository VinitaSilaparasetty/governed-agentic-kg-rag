TITLE: Centrifugal Pump Bearing Wear — Diagnosis and Replacement Procedure
SOURCE: Industrial Maintenance Manual Rev. 4.2, Section 7.3

SECTION 1: SYMPTOM IDENTIFICATION
Bearing wear in centrifugal pumps typically manifests as broadband vibration with elevated
amplitudes in the 1x and sub-synchronous frequency bands. Velocity readings exceeding
6 mm/s RMS at the bearing housing are a primary indicator. Thermal imaging will show
localised hotspots above 80°C at the bearing race location. Audible symptoms include a
characteristic high-pitched whine progressing to a rumbling noise as wear advances.

SECTION 2: ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
Common root causes include: (a) lubrication starvation — inspect grease quantity and
consistency; bearing grease should be replenished every 2000 operating hours for
continuous-duty pumps; (b) misalignment — a misaligned shaft transmits cyclic bending
loads onto the bearing outer race, accelerating spalling; check coupling alignment before
replacing bearings; (c) contamination ingress — inspect bearing housing seals and
labyrinth seals for wear; (d) overloading — verify that pump is operating within the
preferred operating region (POR) of its performance curve, 70–120% of BEP flow.

SECTION 3: REPLACEMENT PROCEDURE
1. Issue LOTO permit. Isolate power at MCC and fit lock and tag to isolator.
2. Allow pump to cool to ambient temperature (<40°C) before opening bearing housing.
3. Remove coupling guard and flexible coupling element. Record coupling gap.
4. Use a hydraulic bearing puller — never impact the inner race directly.
5. Inspect shaft journal surface for scoring (>0.05 mm gouge depth = replace shaft).
6. Clean housing bore and inspect for out-of-round (tolerance ±0.02 mm).
7. Heat replacement bearing to 100°C in an induction heater; slide onto shaft with
   drive force applied only to inner race.
8. Pack housing with correct grease grade (e.g. NLGI 2, lithium complex).
9. Reassemble coupling, torque fasteners to spec. Perform laser alignment.
10. Run pump for 2 hours at reduced load; monitor vibration and temperature trends.

SECTION 4: POST-MAINTENANCE CHECKS
After bearing replacement, establish a 30-day enhanced monitoring schedule:
vibration readings every 48 hours, temperature trending at every shift change.
Update the CMMS work order with bearing serial number and installation date.
