SECTION 1: MEASUREMENT GUIDELINES
Vibration measurements should be taken at each bearing housing location in three axes:
horizontal (H), vertical (V), and axial (A). Use a calibrated accelerometer with a
frequency range suitable for the expected fault — 10 Hz to 10 kHz covers most rotating
machinery faults. Report results in velocity (mm/s RMS) for ISO 10816-3 comparison.

ISO 10816-3 severity zones for large machines (>15 kW, rigid mounting):
  Zone A (new): 0–2.3 mm/s — acceptable
  Zone B (long-term): 2.3–4.5 mm/s — monitor
  Zone C (short-term only): 4.5–7.1 mm/s — investigate; plan maintenance
  Zone D (dangerous): >7.1 mm/s — shut down immediately

SECTION 2: FREQUENCY ANALYSIS
Time-domain overall levels indicate severity; frequency spectra indicate the cause.
Key frequency markers:
- 1x RPM: unbalance, shaft bow, misalignment (radial + axial)
- 2x RPM: misalignment, looseness, resonance
- 0.43–0.48x (sub-synchronous): oil whirl, cavitation onset
- BPFO/BPFI (bearing defect frequencies): early bearing wear
- Broadband 1–20 kHz: cavitation, bearing surface failure, electrical discharge

SECTION 3: COMMON DIAGNOSTIC PATTERNS FOR CENTRIFUGAL PUMPS
VIBRATION + NOISE + HEAT AT BEARING → Bearing Wear (confirm with bearing defect
frequency in spectrum)
VIBRATION + CRACKLING NOISE + FLOW REDUCTION → Cavitation (check NPSHa, inlet conditions)
VIBRATION + AXIAL THRUST + COUPLING HEAT → Misalignment (check laser alignment)
VIBRATION + FLUID AROUND SHAFT → Seal Leakage (inspect mechanical seal)
VIBRATION AT 2x + BEARING OVERLOAD → Rotor Eccentricity or Misalignment

SECTION 4: DECISION TREE FOR PUMP VIBRATION
1. Is vibration >7.1 mm/s? → Shutdown immediately; escalate to maintenance team.
2. Is vibration 4.5–7.1 mm/s?
   a. Check bearing temperature: >80°C → suspect Bearing Wear → Section 7.3
   b. Check inlet for crackling noise → suspect Cavitation → Section 8.1
   c. Check coupling temperature: >70°C → suspect Misalignment → Section 9.2
   d. Check for fluid weep at shaft → suspect Seal Leakage → Section 10.4
3. Is vibration 2.3–4.5 mm/s? → Increase monitoring frequency; trend data; plan inspection.
4. Is vibration <2.3 mm/s? → Normal; continue routine monitoring schedule.

SECTION 5: DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
All vibration measurements must be recorded in the CMMS with: date/time, equipment tag,
measurement point, transducer serial number, overall level, and peak frequency.
Trending must be reviewed monthly by the reliability engineer.
